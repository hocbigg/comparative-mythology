# Comparative Mythology — Advanced Topics

These tracks build directly on the Core Curriculum, moving from foundational knowledge into distinct specializations. Each track represents a genuinely different way of practicing comparative mythology, whether through a specific methodology, a regional corpus, or an applied domain.

---

**Track Index:**

- **Indo-European and Deep Comparative Reconstruction** — The technical reconstruction of prehistoric mythic systems using linguistic, archaeological, and comparative methods.
- **Structural Analysis and the Morphology of Narrative** — Lévi-Straussian structuralism and Proppian formalism as analytical methods for myth and folktales.
- **Myth, Ritual, and Sacrificial Violence** — The myth-ritual complex and the role of sacrifice as a generative mechanism behind mythic narrative.
- **Depth Psychology and Archetypal Analysis** — Jungian and post-Jungian approaches to myth as a manifestation of the collective unconscious.
- **Myth in Modern Literature and Popular Culture** — How mythic patterns operate in, and are consciously deployed by, contemporary storytellers and media creators.

---

## Indo-European and Deep Comparative Reconstruction

The most technically demanding specialization in comparative mythology, using historical linguistics and archaeology to reconstruct mythic systems that predate any written record.

The Dumézilian Framework: [Mitra-Varuna: An Essay on Two Indo-European Representations (Georges Dumézil)](https://ia803201.us.archive.org/30/items/MitraVaruna_202003/) - Dumézil's foundational demonstration of the tripartite ideology (sovereignty, warfare, fertility) underlying Indo-European myth; read this before attempting any other Indo-European comparison.

Comprehensive Indo-European Mythology: [Comparative Mythology (Jaan Puhvel)](https://archive.org/details/comparativemytho0000puhv) - the most systematic single-volume treatment of Indo-European mythic reconstruction, covering Vedic, Iranian, Greek, Roman, Celtic, Germanic, Baltic, and Slavic traditions; use it as the primary reference once you understand Dumézil's framework.

Prehistoric and Global Mythology: [The Origins of the World's Mythologies (E. J. Michael Witzel)](https://archive.org/details/originsofworldsm0000witz) - Witzel's ambitious reconstruction of mythic systems back to 100,000 years ago, distinguishing "Laurasian" (Eurasian and American) from "Gondwana" (African, Melanesian, Australian) mythologies; the most significant theoretical advance in comparative mythology since Campbell.

---

## Structural Analysis and the Morphology of Narrative

The formal analysis of myth and folktale as structured systems, focusing on underlying patterns rather than surface content.

Narrative Morphology: [Morphology of the Folktale, 2nd Edition (Vladimir Propp)](https://monoskop.org/images/f/f3/Propp_Vladimir_Morphology_of_the_Folktale_2nd_ed.pdf) - Propp's identification of 31 narratemes (functional units) that generate all Russian wonder-tales; the foundational text for any structural approach to narrative, and the direct catalyst for Lévi-Strauss's structuralism.

Structural Anthropology of Myth: [Myth and Meaning (Claude Lévi-Strauss)](https://oa.mg/work/10.3138/9781442654112) - five accessible lectures in which Lévi-Strauss explains his method of analyzing myths as systems of binary oppositions; the best entry point into structuralist analysis before tackling his dense four-volume Mythologiques.

Lévi-Strauss's Method Applied: ["The Structural Study of Myth" (Claude Lévi-Strauss)](https://www.people.iup.edu/~mmccallum/lvistrauss.html) - the seminal 1955 essay in which Lévi-Strauss demonstrates his method on the Oedipus myth; read this alongside Propp to see how the two approaches differ.

---

## Myth, Ritual, and Sacrificial Violence

The study of myth as inseparable from ritual practice, particularly sacrifice, with violence as the generative mechanism behind mythic narrative.

The Cambridge Ritualist Foundation: [Prolegomena to the Study of Greek Religion (Jane Ellen Harrison)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6543) - the 1903 work that launched the myth-ritual school, arguing that Greek myths originated as etiological explanations for ritual practices; freely available on Project Gutenberg.

Sacrificial Ritual and Greek Myth: [Homo Necans: The Anthropology of Ancient Greek Sacrificial Ritual and Myth (Walter Burkert)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780520045702) - Burkert's influential argument that Greek sacrificial ritual and its associated myths derive from Paleolithic hunting practices; the most important modern work on the ritual origins of myth.

The Scapegoat Mechanism: [Violence and the Sacred (René Girard)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780801822186) - Girard's theory that all sacred violence originates in the scapegoat mechanism, in which communities resolve internal conflict by collectively blaming and expelling (or killing) a victim; a framework that applies across myth, ritual, and literature.

Girard's Theory in Brief: [René Girard: The Scapegoat Mechanism (Dr. Justin Jackson)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1rLW_LlybgQ) - a free YouTube lecture that clearly explains Girard's mimetic desire and scapegoat mechanism; use this as an accessible entry point before reading Violence and the Sacred.

---

## Depth Psychology and Archetypal Analysis

The Jungian and post-Jungian approach to myth as symbolic communication from the collective unconscious, with archetypes as its structural units.

Jung's Core Theory: [The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious, 2nd Edition (C. G. Jung)](https://ia801406.us.archive.org/30/items/Birdsfrogsandmirrors/Jung%20-%20The%20Archetypes%20and%20the%20Collective%20Unconscious%20%28Collected%20Works%209%29.pdf) - Volume 9 of Jung's Collected Works, in which he defines the archetype concept and its relationship to myth; essential primary-source reading for anyone taking the psychological approach seriously.

Jung's Personal Mythology: [C. G. Jung and the Red Book — Lecture Series (Lance Owens, MD)](http://gnosis.org/jung.html) - a series of in-depth audio lectures on Jung's Red Book (Liber Novus), the private journal in which Jung engaged directly with mythic imagery from his own unconscious; the best available guide to understanding how Jung actually worked with myth rather than just theorized about it.

---

## Myth in Modern Literature and Popular Culture

The study of how mythic patterns are consciously deployed in modern fiction, film, and other media, from Tolkien's mythopoeia to Hollywood's use of the hero's journey.

The Theory of Mythopoeia: ["On Fairy-Stories" (J. R. R. Tolkien)](https://midnightoilstudios.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/On-Fairy-Stories-Tolkien-1.pdf) - Tolkien's 1939 lecture (published 1947) on the nature of fairy-stories, the concept of "sub-creation," and the "eucatastrophe"; the foundational theoretical statement on modern myth-making by the most influential practitioner of the twentieth century.

Modern Myth and Creative Mythology: [The Masks of God, Volume IV: Creative Mythology (Joseph Campbell)](https://archive.org/details/masksofgodcreati00camp) - Campbell's examination of how mythic consciousness has transformed in the modern era, from Dante through Joyce; the final volume of his Masks of God series and the most relevant to contemporary culture.

Applied Hero's Journey: [The Writer's Journey: Mythic Structure for Writers, 3rd Edition (Christopher Vogler)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781935247024) - Vogler's adaptation of Campbell's monomyth into a practical 12-stage framework for screenwriters and novelists; the standard text on applied mythology in professional storytelling, developed from his famous memo at Disney.

## Curation Summary

**Tracks designed and pedagogical justification:**

- **Indo-European and Deep Comparative Reconstruction** — represents the most rigorous and technically demanding methodology in the field; included because it is the only specialization that trains learners to reconstruct unwritten mythic systems from linguistic and archaeological evidence, a skill no other track provides.
- **Structural Analysis and the Morphology of Narrative** — included because structuralism and Proppian formalism are the two dominant formalist methods for analyzing myth and folktale; understanding them is necessary for engaging with most twentieth-century scholarship in the field.
- **Myth, Ritual, and Sacrificial Violence** — included because the myth-ritual complex is one of the oldest and most persistent theoretical traditions in comparative mythology, and the specific question of sacrificial violence (via Burkert and Girard) remains the most active area of contemporary debate.
- **Depth Psychology and Archetypal Analysis** — included because Jungian approaches constitute a distinct and widely practiced specialization, particularly in literature, religious studies, and psychotherapy; it requires its own dedicated track because its methods and evidence differ fundamentally from philological or anthropological approaches.
- **Myth in Modern Literature and Popular Culture** — included because this is the most practically applicable specialization, with direct relevance to creative writing, screenwriting, and media analysis; it bridges academic study of myth with its most common contemporary use.

**Free-vs-paid balance per track:**

- **Indo-European and Deep Comparative Reconstruction** — mostly free (two of three primary resources are freely available on the Internet Archive). I searched for free courses or lecture series specifically on Indo-European mythology and found none that adequately covered this technical material at an advanced level; the field is genuinely book-based.
- **Structural Analysis and the Morphology of Narrative** — mostly free (Propp's PDF on Monoskop, Lévi-Strauss's Myth and Meaning on OA.mg, and "The Structural Study of Myth" essay on a university course page). I checked for free structured courses on structuralist myth analysis and found none; the primary sources are fortunately available as open-access PDFs.
- **Myth, Ritual, and Sacrificial Violence** — mixed. Harrison's Prolegomena is free on Project Gutenberg, and the Girard YouTube lecture is freely accessible. Burkert's Homo Necans and Girard's Violence and the Sacred are paid books; I searched for adequate free equivalents or open-access full-text versions and found none — the Internet Archive has a borrowable copy of Homo Necans but not a freely downloadable one, and Violence and the Sacred is not available in open access.
- **Depth Psychology and Archetypal Analysis** — mostly free (Jung's Collected Works volume 9 is available as a PDF, and the Lance Owens lecture series on the Red Book is freely accessible on Gnosis.org). I checked for free MOOCs or university courses specifically on Jungian approaches to myth and found none adequate.
- **Myth in Modern Literature and Popular Culture** — mixed. Tolkien's essay and Campbell's Creative Mythology are freely available. Vogler's The Writer's Journey is a paid book; I searched for free open-access equivalents on applied hero's journey for writers and found none that matched its practical detail and authority.
