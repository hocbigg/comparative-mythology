# Comparative Mythology — Advanced Topics

These specialization tracks build directly on the Core Curriculum, moving from foundational literacy into focused methodologies, specialized regional reconstructions, and contemporary applications. Learners are expected to choose one or two tracks aligned with their research interests or creative goals rather than attempting to complete all tracks.

**Track Index:**

- **Indo-European and Deep Comparative Reconstruction** — The technical reconstruction of prehistoric mythic systems and formulaic poetics using historical linguistics, archaeology, and comparative philology.
- **Structural Analysis and the Morphology of Narrative** — Formalist, semiotic, and structuralist methodologies for analyzing myth, folktale, and narrative grammar as rule-governed symbolic systems.
- **Myth, Ritual, and Sacrificial Violence** — The generative relationship between mythic narratives and ritual practices, focusing on the anthropological mechanisms of sacred violence and scapegoating.
- **Depth Psychology and Archetypal Analysis** — Jungian and post-Jungian psychological approaches that interpret mythic patterns and deities as symbolic manifestations of the unconscious.
- **Myth in Modern Literature and Popular Culture** — How ancient mythic structures and archetypes are consciously adapted, transformed, and created anew in modern literature, speculative worldbuilding, and cinema.

## Indo-European and Deep Comparative Reconstruction

Investigates the historical-comparative reconstruction of prehistoric mythologies, pantheons, and formulaic poetics through linguistics, archaeology, and comparative philology.

Comparative Indo-European Linguistics: [Introduction to Comparative Indo-European Linguistics (Universiteit Leiden / Tijmen Pronk)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/comparative-indo-european-linguistics) - A free-to-audit eight-module university course providing the technical linguistic foundation of sound laws, language families, and ancestral reconstructive methodology.

The Dumézilian Framework and Tripartite Ideology: [Mitra-Varuna: An Essay on Two Indo-European Representations of Sovereignty (Georges Dumézil)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780942299137) - Dumézil's foundational demonstration of the tripartite ideology (sovereignty, warfare, fertility) and dual divine sovereignty across Indo-European traditions.

Comprehensive Indo-European Mythology: [Comparative Mythology (Jaan Puhvel)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780801839382) - The standard single-volume synthesis applying the comparative method across Vedic, Iranian, Greek, Roman, Celtic, and Germanic mythic traditions.

Indo-European Poetics and Mythic Formulae: [How to Kill a Dragon: Aspects of Indo-European Poetics (Calvert Watkins)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780195144130) - A landmark study reconstructing inherited poetic formulae and the combat mythologem of the dragon-slaying hero across Indo-European daughter languages.

Macro-Comparative and Prehistoric Mythological Phylogeny: [The Origins of the World's Mythologies (E. J. Michael Witzel)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199810123) - Reconstructs mythic systems back to the Paleolithic, distinguishing Laurasian and Gondwana macro-strata through philological and comparative analysis.

## Structural Analysis and the Morphology of Narrative

Explores formalist, semiotic, and structuralist methodologies for analyzing myth, folktale, and narrative grammar as rule-governed symbolic systems.

Russian Formalism and Narrative Morphology: [Morphology of the Folktale (Vladimir Propp)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780292783768) - Propp's foundational identification of thirty-one constant functions and seven character spheres that generate Russian wonder-tales and oral narratives.

Semiotics and Structuralist Literary Theory: [Introduction to Theory of Literature: Semiotics and Structuralism (Yale Open Courses / Paul H. Fry)](https://oyc.yale.edu/english/engl-300/lecture-8) - A recorded university lecture introducing Saussurean semiology, binary opposition, and the transition from linguistic signifiers to structural cultural analysis.

Structural Anthropology and Binary Myth Analysis: [Myth and Meaning: Cracking the Code of Culture (Claude Lévi-Strauss)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780805210385) - Accessible radio lectures in which Lévi-Strauss explains his method of decoding myths as systems of mediating binary oppositions.

Applied Structural Myth Analysis: ["The Structural Study of Myth" in Structural Anthropology (Claude Lévi-Strauss)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780465095162) - Lévi-Strauss's seminal essay demonstrating his structural method on the Oedipus myth by breaking narrative sequence into constituent bundles of relations (mythemes).

## Myth, Ritual, and Sacrificial Violence

Examines the generative relationship between mythic narratives and ritual practices, focusing on the anthropological mechanisms of sacred violence, scapegoating, and blood sacrifice.

The Cambridge Ritualist Foundation: [Prolegomena to the Study of Greek Religion (Jane Ellen Harrison)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6543) - The landmark 1903 study launching the myth-and-ritual school by arguing that Greek mythic narratives originate as spoken etiologies of underlying ritual actions.

Sacrificial Killing and Paleolithic Origins: [Homo Necans: The Anthropology of Ancient Greek Sacrificial Ritual and Myth (Walter Burkert)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780520058750) - Burkert's influential argument tracing Greek sacrificial ritual and associated violence back to Paleolithic hunting rites and collective guilt.

The Scapegoat Mechanism and Cultural Order: [Violence and the Sacred (René Girard)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780801822186) - The foundational text arguing that all human religious systems and mythic institutions originate from the collective expulsion or murder of a surrogate victim.

Mimetic Theory and Mythic Scapegoating Explained: [René Girard: The Scapegoat Mechanism (Dr. Justin Jackson / YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1rLW_LlybgQ) - An in-depth recorded lecture analyzing Girard's mechanics of mimetic desire, cultural crisis, and the unanimous scapegoat mechanism underlying ancient myth.

## Depth Psychology and Archetypal Analysis

Investigates Jungian and post-Jungian psychological approaches that interpret mythic patterns and deities as symbolic projections of the human psyche and collective unconscious.

Foundational Archetypal Theory: [The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious (C. G. Jung)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780691018331) - Volume 9, Part 1 of the Collected Works, defining archetypes, the shadow, the anima/animus, and their manifestation in world mythology.

Active Imagination and Primary Visionary Mythopoesis: [C. G. Jung and the Red Book — Lecture Series (Lance S. Owens / The Gnosis Archive)](http://gnosis.org/redbook/index.html) - A multi-part audio lecture series examining Jung's visionary journal Liber Novus, illuminating how Jung actively experienced and cataloged living mythic imagery.

Cross-Cultural Archetypal Analysis of the Feminine: [The Great Mother: An Analysis of the Archetype (Erich Neumann)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780691166070) - A comparative study examining the primordial archetype of the feminine and maternal across global art, ritual, and ancient myth.

Archetypal Interpretation of Folktale and Myth: [The Interpretation of Fairy Tales (Marie-Louise von Franz)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780877735267) - A definitive analytical guide demonstrating the methodology of interpreting shadow, anima, and persona motifs within traditional oral tales.

## Myth in Modern Literature and Popular Culture

Explores how ancient mythic structures and archetypes are consciously adapted, transformed, and created anew in modern literature, speculative fiction, and popular narrative media.

The Theory of Mythopoeia and Sub-creation: [Tolkien On Fairy-Stories (J. R. R. Tolkien / ed. Verlyn Flieger & Douglas A. Anderson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780007582914) - Tolkien's definitive essay outlining sub-creation, Secondary Worlds, and eucatastrophe, establishing the theoretical framework for deliberate modern mythmaking.

Archetypal Criticism and the Mythos of Literary Genres: [Anatomy of Criticism: Four Essays (Northrop Frye)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780691202563) - Frye's landmark work of literary taxonomy, specifically the third essay classifying literary modes into four archetypal seasonal mythoi (comedy, romance, tragedy, and irony).

Creative Mythology and Secular Mythopoesis: [The Masks of God, Volume IV: Creative Mythology (Joseph Campbell)](https://archive.org/details/masksofgodcreati00camp) - Campbell's study of the historical transition from dogmatic communal myth to the individualized, creative mythologies forged by modern artists and writers.

Applied Monomyth in Storytelling and Screenwriting: [The Writer's Journey: Mythic Structure for Writers, 4th Edition (Christopher Vogler)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781615933150) - The standard creative writing and cinematic adaptation of Campbell's hero's journey into a twelve-stage practical story-crafting framework.
