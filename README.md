# Comparative Mythology — Core Curriculum

This curriculum covers the foundational subjects that anyone studying comparative mythology needs before specializing. The four subjects progress from broad definitions and surveys, through the two most influential mythological corpora, to the theoretical frameworks that make comparison possible.

## Introduction to Mythology

Covers what myth is, how it differs from religion, folklore, and legend, the basic typology of myths (creation, hero, trickster, apocalypse), and a survey of major world mythological traditions to establish a comparative baseline.

[Crash Course World Mythology (CrashCourse / Mike Rugnetta)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLEb6sGT7oD8G8nPbyvObaZUNdfV6kitZQ) - a free 41-episode YouTube series that spans world mythologies thematically (creation myths, the hero's journey, tricksters, apocalyptic myths, etc.); use it as your primary structured introduction, watching episodes in order.

[World Mythology: An Anthology of the Great Myths and Epics, Donna Rosenberg](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780844259666) - a paid college-level anthology organized by region (Greece, Rome, the Middle East, Northern Europe, the British Isles, Africa, Asia, and the Americas); use it alongside the video series if you want primary-text readings to accompany the survey, but it is a supplement rather than a requirement.

---

## Classical Mythology

Covers the Greek and Roman mythological tradition in depth, including the Olympian pantheon, the heroic cycles, the major literary sources (Hesiod, Homer, the tragedians, Ovid), and the social and religious contexts of these myths.

[Mythology Unbound: An Online Textbook for Classical Mythology (Jessica Mellenthin & Susan O. Shapiro)](https://uen.pressbooks.pub/mythologyunbound/) - a free, openly licensed textbook with short, readable chapters on all the major Greek myths and figures; this is your primary reference for the Greek material.

[Metamorphoses, Ovid (trans. Garth, Dryden, et al.)](https://classics.mit.edu/Ovid/metam.html) - a free full-text translation of Ovid's epic on the MIT Internet Classics Archive; read selections (at minimum Books I, VIII, and XV) as primary-source material alongside the textbook.

[Hesiod, Theogony (trans. H. G. Evelyn-White)](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HesiodTheogony.html) - a free full-text translation of the standard Greek creation myth on the Theoi Classical Texts Library; read this early in your study as the foundational Greek cosmogony.

[The Ancient Greek Hero (HarvardX / Gregory Nagy)](https://www.edx.org/course/the-ancient-greek-hero) - a free, archived Harvard MOOC (18 weeks) covering the concept of the hero in Greek literature from Homer through Plato; audit the archived course materials at your own pace for a deeper, university-level treatment of how Greek myth operated in its literary and ritual contexts.

---

## Major World Mythologies

Covers the key mythological traditions beyond Greece and Rome that a comparativist needs for meaningful cross-cultural analysis, with particular emphasis on the Norse tradition (the most commonly studied non-classical corpus) and the ancient Near East.

[Old Norse Mythology in the Sources (University of Colorado Boulder / Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/old-norse-mythology-sources) - a free, beginner-level Coursera course (approximately 6 hours) on the written sources of Viking-Age religion, including the Poetic Edda, Snorri's Edda, and Saxo; audit the full course for free.

[The Prose Edda, Snorri Sturluson (trans. Arthur Gilchrist Brodeur)](https://sacred-texts.com/neu/pre/index.htm) - a free full-text translation of the primary handbook of Norse mythology on the Internet Sacred Text Archive; read this as your main primary source for the Norse tradition.

[The Poetic Edda (trans. Henry Adams Bellows)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/73533) - a free full-text translation of the collection of Old Norse mythological and heroic poems on Project Gutenberg; read selections (especially the Völuspá and Hávamál) alongside the Prose Edda to get the poetic sources behind Snorri's prose.

For the Mesopotamian tradition, the Crash Course World Mythology series (listed above) includes episodes on the Epic of Gilgamesh and Babylonian creation myths, and free full-text translations of Gilgamesh are widely available online (search "Epic of Gilgamesh full text" on the Internet Sacred Text Archive or Project Gutenberg).

## Theories of Myth

Covers the major scholarly frameworks for interpreting and comparing myths across cultures, including the psychological (Freud, Jung), anthropological (Tylor, Frazer, Malinowski), structuralist (Lévi-Strauss), ritualist (Harrison, Hooke), and archetypal (Campbell, Eliade) approaches.

[Myth: A Very Short Introduction, Robert A. Segal](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780198724704) - a paid but inexpensive (around $12) book that systematically applies each major theory of myth to the same myth (Adonis), letting you compare approaches directly; this is the best single starting point for theory and is worth the cost given the absence of a comparable free overview.

[The Golden Bough, James George Frazer](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3623) - the free 1922 abridged edition of Frazer's landmark comparative study of mythology and religion on Project Gutenberg; read the opening chapters and selected sections rather than attempting the whole book, as it is long and many of its conclusions are now outdated, but it remains the foundational text of comparative mythology.

[Joseph Campbell and The Power of Myth with Bill Moyers (PBS)](https://www.pbs.org/show/joseph-campbell-and-the-power-of-myth-with-bill-moyers) - the complete six-episode PBS interview series in which Campbell presents his comparative approach to mythology and the hero's journey; the series is also freely available on YouTube (search "Power of Myth PBS").

[Magic, Science, and Religion (MIT OpenCourseWare, 21A.520)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21a-520-magic-science-and-religion-fall-2021) - a free MIT anthropology course examining the relationship between magic, science, and religion as belief systems; use the readings and lecture materials to understand the anthropological context in which myths function, particularly the work of Tylor, Frazer, and Malinowski.

# Comparative Mythology — Core Curriculum

This curriculum covers the foundational subjects required for systematic comparative study across cultures. The subjects progress from universal definitions and structural typologies, through the foundational Mediterranean and non-Mediterranean mythic corpora, to the modern theoretical frameworks that make rigorous cross-cultural analysis possible.

## Introduction to Mythology

Covers foundational definitions distinguishing myth from folklore, legend, and religion, alongside recurring cross-cultural archetypes including creation myths, flood narratives, tricksters, and heroic journeys.

[Crash Course World Mythology (CrashCourse / Mike Rugnetta)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL8dPuuaLjXtNCG9Vq7vdvJytS-F-xGi7_) - Primary 41-episode video lecture series that introduces global archetypes, motifs, and comparative structures across world traditions.

[World Mythology: An Anthology of the Great Myths and Epics (Donna Rosenberg)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780844259666) - Serves as a complementary primary-source reading anthology to accompany the video lectures, providing full regional texts from Africa, the Americas, Asia, and Europe.

## Classical Mythology

Explores the ancient Greek and Roman mythological traditions in depth, covering Olympian pantheons, cosmogony, heroic cycles, and the literary transmission of myth in classical antiquity.

[Greek and Roman Mythology (University of Pennsylvania / Peter Struck)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/mythology) - Primary structured lecture course guiding learners through Greek and Roman mythic texts, religious context, and modern interpretative frameworks; audit the course for free.

[Mythology Unbound: An Online Textbook for Classical Mythology (Jessica Mellenthin & Susan O. Shapiro)](https://uen.pressbooks.pub/mythologyunbound/) - An open-access reading textbook providing deity-by-deity reference profiles and narrative overviews that complement the video lectures.

[Theogony (Hesiod / trans. H. G. Evelyn-White)](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HesiodTheogony.html) - Foundational Greek primary text for cosmogony and divine succession; read this early alongside the course modules on creation myths.

[Metamorphoses (Ovid / trans. Sir Samuel Garth, John Dryden, et al.)](https://classics.mit.edu/Ovid/metam.html) - Canonical Roman epic compilation of transformation myths; read selected books (such as Books I, VIII, and XV) as complementary primary literature following the Greek texts.

## Comparative World Corpora: Ancient Near East and Northern Europe

Examines key non-classical comparative corpora through ancient Mesopotamian and Old Norse narrative sources, focusing on creation myths, divine conflicts, the heroic quest for immortality, and apocalyptic eschatology.

[Old Norse Mythology in the Sources (University of Colorado Boulder / Mathias Nordvig)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/old-norse-mythology-sources) - Structured lecture course surveying the historical and literary transmission of pre-Christian Scandinavian myths; audit the course for free alongside the primary source readings.

[The Epic of Gilgamesh (trans. Maureen Gallery Kovacs)](https://www.ancienttexts.org/library/mesopotamian/gilgamesh/) - Core ancient Near Eastern primary text on mortality, friendship, and the flood motif, providing a free full-text translation of the standard Babylonian epic.

[The Epic of Gilgamesh (trans. Andrew George)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780140449198) - An authoritative modern verse translation with comprehensive introductory analysis; read as a modern print alternative to the free Kovacs edition above.

[The Younger Edda (Snorri Sturluson / trans. Rasmus B. Anderson)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/18947) - The primary prose handbook of Old Norse mythology; read this first among the Scandinavian texts to understand the cosmological architecture and divine narratives.

[The Poetic Edda (trans. Henry Adams Bellows)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/73533) - The older poetic source collection in alliterative verse; read key poems such as Völuspá and Hávamál as a poetic complement to Snorri's prose handbook.

## Theories of Myth

Surveys major modern scholarly frameworks for interpreting and comparing myths across cultures, including psychological, structuralist, functionalist, ritualist, and archetypal approaches.

[Myth: A Very Short Introduction (Robert A. Segal)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780198724704) - Core introductory textbook that systematically compares and tests all major modern theories of myth against a single test narrative.

[The Golden Bough (James George Frazer)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3623) - Historical primary text of the comparative ritualist and "dying god" school; read selected introductory chapters alongside Segal's modern critique rather than tackling the entire volume.

[Joseph Campbell and The Power of Myth with Bill Moyers (PBS)](https://www.pbs.org/show/joseph-campbell-and-the-power-of-myth-with-bill-moyers) - A six-part video interview series serving as an accessible audiovisual exploration of the monomyth and archetypal theory to complement Segal's academic analysis.
